Medical informatics can sound like a computing subject, so let me be clear about what it is and what it isn't.

Medical informatics is the use of data, information and systems to support healthcare and laboratory work. It is not IT support. Fixing a printer or updating a laptop is not what we mean.

Think of three everyday examples. First, a laboratory tracking specimens: every tube must be linked to the right request, so the result goes back to the right patient. Second, a clinician viewing a result: the value only helps if it arrives with its units, its reference interval and any comments. Third, a clinic team looking at a year of HbA1c results to see how well its diabetes care is working.

In each case the question is the same: is the information reliable, can the right person find it, and is it safe to use?

For you as biomedical scientists, that matters directly. You produce laboratory results. Medical informatics is about what happens to those results once they leave the analyser — and how to stop a good measurement turning into a poor decision.
