Patient information needs protecting in three different ways, and it helps to keep them separate.

Confidentiality means only the people who need the information for their role can see it. It doesn't mean nobody sees it — a result that no one can see is no use. It means the right people, and only them.

Integrity means the information is accurate, complete and attached to the right patient. A perfectly measured potassium filed in the wrong record has lost its integrity.

Availability means the information is there when it is needed. If the laboratory system goes down and the doctor can't see a result while deciding on care, the result exists but isn't available.

Each of these maps back to the journey you've just studied, and we'll use them again when we look at access in Week 2 and at governance in Week 8.
