Let's work through one hand-off in detail: the specimen leaving the clinic and arriving in the laboratory.

First, what is created at collection? Labelled tubes, the time of collection, and the identity of the person who collected the blood.

Second, what travels? The specimens, together with the request.

Third, what does the laboratory check on arrival? That the label matches the request, that the specimen is suitable for the tests asked for, and the time it was received.

Fourth, what can go wrong? A wrong label is an error. A late arrival is a delay. A missing collection time is a loss — the information simply isn't there when someone needs it.

And fifth, the consequence. If the specimen is from the wrong patient, or unsuitable, the analyser can do its job perfectly and still produce a result that should not be trusted.

That's the heart of this week's question. Reliability doesn't come from the analyser alone; it depends on every step before and after it. On the next screen, you'll try the same reasoning on a different hand-off.
