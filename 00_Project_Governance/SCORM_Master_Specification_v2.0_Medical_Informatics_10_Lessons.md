# 3. LEARNER PROFILE

The learners are final-year Biomedical Science students.

They are NOT Computer Science students.

Students are expected to have prior knowledge of:

- Cell biology
- Genetics
- Anatomy and physiology
- Microbiology
- Biochemistry
- Molecular biology
- Basic statistics
- Research methods
- Laboratory techniques
- Bioinformatics
- Data interpretation

The SCORM packages must therefore:

1. Build on biomedical knowledge.
2. Introduce computing/informatics concepts progressively.
3. Avoid assuming programming expertise.
4. Explain technical concepts through biomedical and healthcare examples.
5. Emphasise application rather than computer-science theory.

---

# 4. OVERALL EDUCATIONAL PURPOSE

Medical Informatics should enable Biomedical Science students to understand how information, data, digital technologies, computational methods, and artificial intelligence support modern healthcare and biomedical practice.

Students should progressively move from:

**Understanding digital health**

→ **working with healthcare data**

→ **interpreting information**

→ **evaluating digital systems**

→ **using computational/AI tools responsibly**

→ **applying informatics thinking to biomedical and healthcare problems**

→ **integrating these capabilities into professional practice.**

# 6. PAIR FRAMEWORK

GenAI activities must use the LSCT PAIR framework where appropriate.

**PAIR =**

### P — Problem
Students identify or analyse a meaningful biomedical/healthcare problem.

### A — AI Tool
Students use an appropriate GenAI or AI-enabled tool.

### I — Interaction
Students interrogate, challenge, refine, compare, or otherwise interact critically with AI output.

### R — Reflection
Students reflect on:

- Accuracy
- Limitations
- Bias
- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Professional implications
- What the AI did well
- What the AI did poorly
- What the student ultimately decided

PAIR activities must not become simple:

> "Ask ChatGPT this question and copy the answer."

---

# 7. 3As FRAMEWORK

Where relevant, activities should demonstrate the LSCT 3As framework:

### Anchor
Students establish disciplinary knowledge, reasoning, evidence, and professional judgement.

### Augment
AI/GenAI is used to extend, accelerate, compare, explain, or support student work.

### Advance
Students use AI and informatics capabilities to perform higher-level professional tasks that would otherwise be difficult or time-consuming.

The student remains responsible for evaluating and applying the output.

# 17. NARRATION

# 18. MANDATORY AUDIO CONTROL

This is a NON-NEGOTIABLE requirement.

If:

`narration_enabled = true`

the screen MUST display a visible lecturer narration component.

The audio component must NOT disappear merely because an MP3 file has not yet been supplied.

---

# 19. AUDIO STATES

Every narration-enabled screen must support three states.

## State A — Audio available

Display:

- Play/pause
- Progress/seek control
- Current time
- Duration where available
- Volume control
- Transcript control

## State B — Audio file missing

The narration component remains visible.

Display:

**LECTURER NARRATION**

**Narration will be available here.**

The play/progress controls may be visibly disabled.

The transcript must remain accessible.

## State C — Audio error

Display:

**Narration is currently unavailable. You may continue with the text content.**

The transcript must remain accessible.

An audio error must never prevent learning or navigation.

---

# 20. AUDIO MANIFEST

Each screen must use a stable screen ID.

Example:

`W01_S01`

Audio path:

`audio/week01/W01_S01.mp3`

Transcript path:

`transcripts/week01/W01_S01.txt`

Example manifest:

```json
{
  "W01_S01": {
    "narration_enabled": true,
    "audio": "audio/week01/W01_S01.mp3",
    "transcript": "transcripts/week01/W01_S01.txt",
    "status": "missing"
  }
}
```

The rendering logic must first evaluate:

`narration_enabled`

and only then determine whether the audio file exists.

Never use:

`audio_exists → render audio`

as the rendering condition.

Correct logic:

`narration_enabled → render audio component → determine audio state`

# 49. Cross-Lesson Narrative & Knowledge Continuity

## 49.1 Principle

The SCORM module must maintain explicit continuity of student knowledge across Lessons 01–10.

Claude must distinguish between:

* **what exists in the overall module architecture**, and
* **what students have actually been introduced to in previous weeks**.

The fact that a person, patient, case, scenario, dataset, organisation, system, concept, or example appears somewhere in the overall module specification does NOT mean that students already know it.

An entity may be treated as previously known only if it was explicitly introduced in an earlier completed week.

## 49.2 First-Introduction Rule

When an entity appears for the first time in a week, it must be introduced as NEW to the learner.

Do not use language that implies prior exposure, including but not limited to:

* "again"
* "once again"
* "reopening"
* "revisiting"
* "returning to"
* "continuing with"
* "back to"
* "as we saw earlier"
* "as discussed previously"
* "as you may remember"
* "recall"
* "remember Ms/Mr..."
* "building on our earlier case of..."
* "let's look at the case again"

unless the referenced entity was explicitly introduced in an earlier week.

Appropriate first-introduction language may include:

* "Meet Ms Farah..."
* "Let's examine Ms Farah's case..."
* "Consider the following case..."
* "We will now look at a new patient scenario..."
* "For this week's case, we will examine..."

## 49.3 Cross-Week Entity Tracking

Before generating each lesson SCORM package, Claude must determine the prior-introduction status of important recurring entities.

For each person, patient, case, scenario, dataset, organisation, system, or major example, classify it as:

1. **NEW** — first introduction in the current week.
2. **PREVIOUSLY INTRODUCED** — explicitly introduced in an earlier week.
3. **RECURRING** — deliberately carried forward from an earlier week.
4. **UNCERTAIN** — insufficient evidence that students have previously encountered it.

If classified as NEW or UNCERTAIN, Claude must NOT use language implying prior knowledge.

If classified as PREVIOUSLY INTRODUCED or RECURRING, continuity language may be used only when supported by the earlier week's content.

## 49.4 No Assumed Prior Knowledge

Claude must not infer student familiarity from:

* the Master Specification
* the 12-Week Blueprint
* the module syllabus
* the lecturer's source materials
* Claude's own knowledge of previous generated packages
* an entity appearing in a later section of the same document
* an entity appearing in another week that was never explicitly introduced

Student-facing continuity must be based on the actual instructional sequence.

## 49.5 Mandatory Continuity Check

Before finalising every lesson SCORM package, Claude must perform a **Cross-Week Narrative Continuity Check**.

The check must identify:

* people/patients introduced for the first time
* cases/scenarios introduced for the first time
* recurring entities carried forward from earlier weeks
* references to "again", "revisit", "reopening", "returning", "continuing", "as discussed earlier", or equivalent wording
* any statement that assumes students possess knowledge that has not yet been explicitly taught

Any unsupported implication of prior knowledge must be corrected before the package is finalised.

## 49.6 Example

INCORRECT:

**"Reopening Ms Farah's Case"**

when Ms Farah is introduced for the first time in Week 04.

CORRECT:

**"Meet Ms Farah: A New Patient Case"**

or:

**"Examining Ms Farah's Case"**

If Ms Farah was explicitly introduced in Week 03 and deliberately carried into Week 04, then wording such as:

**"Returning to Ms Farah's Case"**

may be appropriate.

## 49.7 Priority

This continuity rule applies to ALL Lessons 01–10 and takes precedence over stylistic attempts to make a lesson appear conversational or engaging.

Claude must prioritise **accurate learner-facing continuity over narrative flair**.
