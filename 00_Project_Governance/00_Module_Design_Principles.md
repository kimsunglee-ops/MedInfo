# Medical Informatics — NP BMS Module Design Context

## 1. Module identity

Institution: Ngee Ann Polytechnic, Singapore  
School: School of Life Sciences & Chemical Technology  
Programme: Diploma in Biomedical Science  
Target cohort: Year 3 / final-year students  
Module: Medical Informatics  
Module duration: 10 teaching weeks  
Weekly guided learning: 2 hours online asynchronous learning and 2 hours practical/applied learning.

## 2. Educational purpose

The module prepares biomedical science graduates to understand how laboratory, clinical and patient-generated data are produced, managed, exchanged, interpreted and used in healthcare.

Students should be able to identify information-quality problems, explain the roles of healthcare information systems, evaluate data exchange and analytical outputs, understand the responsible use of AI and digital health technologies, analyse information-governance risks, and recommend improvements to biomedical or healthcare workflows.

The module is not intended to train software developers or IT specialists.

## 3. Central question

How does a test order become a reliable laboratory result, reach the right healthcare professional, and support an appropriate decision?

## 4. Student-facing conceptual model

TEST ORDER → HEALTHCARE SYSTEMS → LABORATORY DATA → VALIDATION → INFORMATION EXCHANGE → INSIGHT AND DECISION → TRUST AND GOVERNANCE → IMPLEMENTATION AND IMPROVEMENT

This model should recur in diagrams, cases, practicals and integration activities.

## 5. Target students and prerequisites

Students have prior learning in cell biology, molecular biology, biochemistry, microbiology, human anatomy and physiology, biomedical laboratory techniques, basic statistics, research methods and introductory bioinformatics.

Instruction should build on this knowledge. Explain unfamiliar informatics terminology clearly, use authentic biomedical examples and avoid unnecessary programming or advanced mathematical content.

## 6. Approved 10-week working sequence

1. Introduction to Medical Informatics and the Healthcare Information Ecosystem
2. Healthcare Information Systems and Clinical Workflows
3. Laboratory Data: Generation, Quality, Meaning and Context
4. Laboratory Information Management, Interoperability and Healthcare Data Exchange
5. Laboratory Result Validation and Reporting Safety
6. Healthcare Data Analytics, Visualisation and Decision Support
7. Digital Health and Remote Healthcare
8. Healthcare Data Privacy, Cybersecurity, Ethics and Governance
9. Singapore's Healthcare Digital Ecosystem: Validation, Implementation and Transformation
10. Integration and Application of Medical Informatics

Use the exact topic titles in the approved syllabus. This sequence is the working baseline and must not be changed without explicit approval.

## 7. Pedagogical progression

Weeks 1–2: Understand the healthcare information ecosystem and the roles of healthcare information systems.

Weeks 3–5: Examine the laboratory information lifecycle, data quality, interoperability, individual-result validation and reporting safety.

Week 6: Interpret data and evaluate analytics and decision-support outputs.

Weeks 7–8: Explore digital health and the responsible use and protection of healthcare information.

Week 9: Connect the Singapore healthcare digital ecosystem to validation, implementation and transformation.

Week 10: Integrate concepts to evaluate and improve an authentic workflow.

## 8. Longitudinal case

Use a fictional patient and laboratory journey, progressing through:
- Test ordering
- Specimen collection and registration
- Laboratory testing and data capture
- Result validation and reporting
- Transfer into clinical information systems
- Interpretation and decision support
- Follow-up or remote monitoring
- Data protection and implementation considerations

Each week adds a new perspective or problem. Do not force every topic into the case when a separate example is more suitable.

## 9. Learning design principles

- Start with the healthcare problem, then explain the technology.
- Introduce the overall system before individual components.
- Use the laboratory as the principal entry point for informatics concepts.
- Progress from explanation to application, analysis, evaluation and integration.
- Keep OAL and practical learning complementary.
- Embed formative checks and feedback within the planned workload.
- Use fictional data and explicit verification of AI outputs.
- Integrate privacy and confidentiality from the beginning.
- Prioritise student reasoning and professional judgement.

## 10. Singapore context

Use relevant examples involving MOH, Synapxe, NEHR, HealthHub and Singapore healthcare data-protection requirements.

Verify current legal, regulatory and implementation details against authoritative sources before teaching. Distinguish national services, technology organisations and individual hospital information systems accurately.
