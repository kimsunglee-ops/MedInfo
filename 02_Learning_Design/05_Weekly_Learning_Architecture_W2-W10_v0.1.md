## 1. Standard weekly template (from `04` §2)
 
Each week specifies content for these fixed segments. Timings are ⚑ planning estimates.
 
**OAL (120 min):** S1 Case update & guiding question (5) · S2 Retrieval, 2–3 items (5) · S3 Core explanation, 2–3 narrated segments ≤ 8 min each (35) · S4 Worked example (15) · S5 Interactive activity (20) · S6 Curated reading (15) · S7 Formative quiz, 6–10 items (15) · S8 Practical preparation (10).
 
**Practical (120 min, face-to-face, ≤ 25 students, groups of 3–4):** P1 Entry check (10) · P2 Briefing (10) · P3 Guided task (35) · P4 Checkpoint (10) · P5 Independent task incl. privacy decision (35) · P6 Debrief (15) · P7 Exit ticket (5).
 
**Quiz standard:** ungraded, ≥ 2 attempts, ≥ 1 item per WLO, ~⅓ at Apply or above, 2 retrieval items from earlier weeks. Full items (answer, distractor rationale, feedback, misconception, remediation) are produced in Stage C.

## 4. Weekly architectures
 
### Week 2 — Healthcare Information Systems and Clinical Workflows
 
**1. Role.** Adds the *systems layer* to the W1 people-and-risk journey. Prepares W3 (identifiers become data-quality items), W4 (LIS detail, interfaces) and W5 (identity check before release). **Lens:** identifiers as a workflow control.
 
**2. Central Question.** Where in Patient A's journey do information systems create, check or move information, and what happens when one fails?
 
**3. Big Idea.** Information systems do not just store data; they embed workflow controls. When information is wrong, missing, late or unavailable, the control fails and risk moves downstream to the patient.
 
**4. WLOs**
 
| WLO | MLO | Level | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Describe the roles of common healthcare information systems and distinguish their principal functions | MLO2 | Understand | OAL matching |
| 2.2 Explain how information systems support different stages of a clinical and laboratory workflow | MLO2 | Understand | Systems-layer map |
| 2.3 Analyse how incorrect, missing, delayed or unavailable information can affect laboratory operations and patient safety | MLO2; MLO1 | Analyse | Failure-mode analysis |
| 2.4 Identify appropriate information-system controls for reducing common workflow errors | MLO2; MLO5 | Apply | Failure-mode analysis |
 
**5. Prerequisites.** W1 journey map; data/information/knowledge; confidentiality–integrity–availability. Prior BMS: specimen labelling and reception practice.
 
**Key concepts (5):** functional roles of EHR, HIS and LIS · electronic ordering and specimen registration · patient identification as a control (two-identifier rule as generic good practice; verify local policy) · role-based access · downtime and continuity procedures.
 
**6. GenAI.** None. Anchor only.
 
**7. PAIR.** Not applicable.
 
**8. OAL**
 
| Seg | Content |
|---|---|
| S1 | Case: order placed electronically; specimen labelled. Guiding question |
| S2 | W1 retrieval: data vs information; journey stages; risk types (error, delay, loss); availability scenario from W1-LO5-S2 as the bridge to downtime |
| S3 | (a) Systems by function, placed on the W1 journey · (b) orders, registration and identifiers as controls · (c) access roles and downtime |
| S4 | Worked example: trace one fictional order, marking *enter / retrieve / transfer / check* points (models P3) |
| S5 | System-function matching + drag control points onto a workflow |
| S6 | Short authoritative source on patient identification (e.g. WHO Patient Safety Solutions; verify currency) |
| S7 | 8 items: 2.1 ×3, 2.2 ×2, 2.3 ×2, 2.4 ×1 + 2 retrieval |
| S8 | Bring W1 map; roles; success criteria |
 
**9. Practical.** *Scenario:* groups keep their W1 role cards (ordering doctor, phlebotomist/clinic nurse, medical technologist, clinic administrator). Each group also gets an **IT/LIS analyst prompt card**: a perspective card, not an extra role, so groups of 3 still work. Each group's retained W1 A3 map is returned. *P3:* complete **row G (Systems)** across all 10 W1 stages, marking each interaction as enter / retrieve / transfer / check; lecturer models stage 1. *P5:* each group analyses one allocated failure (mislabelled identifier; wrong test request; result not visible to recipient; LIS down 2 h at peak receipt) so all four are covered in class. Each failure is classified with the **W1 risk types** (error, delay, loss), then traced: consequence → detection point → one control (prevent / detect / recover). *Privacy decision (new lens):* W1 decided **who needs to see** the values; W2 assigns **system permissions** (view / enter / amend / release) for two roles and justifies one restriction. *P6:* compare failures across groups; link forward to W3. **Primary artefact:** annotated systems-layer map with one failure-mode analysis.
*Success criteria:* every stage in row G shows ≥ 1 correctly typed system interaction · failure classified by risk type and consequence traced to lab and patient impact · control is specific, located on the map and typed · permission restriction justified by role, not job title.
 
**10. Formative.** Exit ticket: "Name one point on your map where a wrong patient identifier would be caught, and the person or system that catches it." *Target misconceptions:* the EHR is where the test is performed; electronic ordering eliminates errors; downtime is only an IT problem.
 
**11. Singapore context.** Systems named generically; no vendor claims (§8 item 10). Reinforce the W1 distinction: an institution's EHR is not NEHR.
 
**12. BMS relevance.** Specimen rejection for labelling errors; downtime request forms; LIS user roles seen on placement.
 
**13. Continuity.** ← W1 map (extended, not repeated). → W3 identifiers as data; W4 LIS/LIMS and interfaces; W5 identity check.
 
**14. Alignment review.** **Green.** All WLOs evidenced; MLO5 supported through the access decision.

### Week 3 — Laboratory Data: Generation, Quality, Meaning and Context
 
**1. Role.** Shifts from the flow to the data itself. First flawed-data inquiry and first AI Augment. Feeds C1. **Lens:** quality within one dataset.
 
**2. Central Question.** When Patient A's results appear in the laboratory dataset, how do we know they are good enough to use?
 
**3. Big Idea.** A number is not a result until its specimen, method, units, reference interval and provenance are known. Electronic data inherit every upstream error.
 
**4. WLOs**
 
| WLO | MLO | Level | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Classify common biomedical and healthcare data according to their characteristics, intended use and lifecycle stage | MLO1 | Understand | OAL check; feeds C1.6 |
| 3.2 Evaluate laboratory data for selected quality problems… | MLO1 | Evaluate | Data-quality audit |
| 3.3 Explain how specimen characteristics, testing methods, units and reference intervals influence interpretation | MLO1 | Understand | Data-quality audit |
| 3.4 Distinguish generation of an analytical result from its validation and clinical interpretation | MLO1; MLO2 | Analyse | OAL check |
 
G2 accepted 12 Nov 2026: 3.1 now includes lifecycle stage. Because lifecycle is now assessed (C1.6), the "overview only" boundary becomes "taught and checked at Understand level".
 
**5. Prerequisites.** Pre-/analytical/post-analytical phases and haemolysis (lab techniques, biochemistry); SI units; W2 identifiers.
 
**Key concepts (5):** data types and lifecycle stage (generation, clinical use, retention, archiving, secondary use) · testing-process error phases · six data-quality dimensions · provenance and metadata (specimen, method, units, reference interval) · generation vs validation vs interpretation.
 
**6. GenAI.** Augment, after the independent audit. Lecturer models one verification in S4 (`04` §8).
 
**7. PAIR.** As §2. Verification record required; students list AI claims that were wrong.
 
**8. OAL**
 
| Seg | Content |
|---|---|
| S1 | Case: Patient A's results appear in the dataset with a haemolysis comment |
| S2 | W2 retrieval: system roles; controls |
| S3 | (a) Data types and lifecycle · (b) where errors arise across phases · (c) quality dimensions, context and metadata |
| S4 | Audit 5 rows by dimension; correct-vs-escalate reasoning; modelled AI verification |
| S5 | Classify seeded flaws by quality dimension |
| S6 | Short source on data-quality dimensions (e.g. DAMA UK six dimensions; verify) |
| S7 | 8 items incl. Apply-level classification and ≥ 1 lifecycle-stage item (G2) |
| S8 | Role, dataset preview, success criteria, AI rules |
 
**9. Practical.** *Role:* laboratory data-quality officer. *Materials:* ~40-row synthetic dataset incl. Patient A, seeded with missing specimen IDs, duplicates, mixed units (e.g. HbA1c % vs mmol/mol), date-format errors, implausible values, inconsistent test names, missing context. *P3:* audit first 10 rows together. *P5:* complete audit with correct-from-verified-source vs escalate decisions; *privacy decision:* identify and justify removing one unneeded identifier, framed as a lifecycle question (the audit is a secondary use of the data, not direct care); 10-min AI Augment step with verification record. **Primary artefact:** data-quality audit with justifications.
*Success criteria:* each flaw classified by dimension · consequence stated · correct/escalate decision justified from a verified source · no value "corrected" by guessing · AI claims checked.
 
**10. Formative.** Exit ticket: "A result row has lost its haemolysis comment. Which quality dimension is affected, and do you correct or escalate?" *Misconceptions:* out-of-range means erroneous; units can be inferred and fixed; deleting duplicates is always safe.
 
**11. Singapore context.** Unit conventions (SI; HbA1c reporting format) to verify locally. Synthetic identifiers must not resemble NRIC format (`04` §10).
 
**12. BMS relevance.** Haemolysis index, specimen acceptance criteria, metadata in instrument output.
 
**13. Continuity.** ← W2 identifiers. → W4 the same problems *between* systems; W5 haemolysed potassium decision; C1.
 
**14. Alignment review.** **Amber:** spreadsheet and AI tool approval (I8). G2 resolved.

### Week 4 — Laboratory Information Management, Interoperability and Healthcare Data Exchange
 
**1. Role.** From one dataset to meaning across systems; introduces LIS vs LIMS and NEHR as a destination. Feeds C1. **Lens:** meaning lost or preserved between systems.
 
**2. Central Question.** When Patient A's result leaves the laboratory, does it arrive with the same meaning?
 
**3. Big Idea.** Transmission is not interoperability. Exchange succeeds only when identity, units, codes and context survive the journey.
 
**4. WLOs**
 
| WLO | MLO | Level | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 Explain the principal functions of LISs and LIMSs and distinguish their typical settings | MLO2 | Understand | OAL check |
| 4.2 Describe how laboratory information may be exchanged between instruments, laboratory systems, clinical systems and shared records such as NEHR | MLO2 | Understand | OAL exchange diagram |
| 4.3 Distinguish levels of interoperability | MLO2 | Analyse | Interoperability table |
| 4.4 Analyse how mismatched identifiers, units, codes or formats affect reliability and usability | MLO2; MLO1 | Analyse | Interoperability table |
 
**5. Prerequisites.** W2 system roles; W3 quality dimensions and metadata.
 
**Key concepts (5):** LIS vs LIMS · interfaces (instrument → LIS → EHR → shared record) · interoperability levels (cite framework, e.g. HIMSS foundational/structural/semantic/organisational; verify) · example standards read-only (HL7 v2 result message, FHIR Observation, LOINC) · NEHR as an exchange destination under the HIA.
 
**6–7. GenAI / PAIR.** None.
 
**8. OAL**
 
| Seg | Content |
|---|---|
| S1 | Case: result sent to clinic EHR and shared record; discrepancies appear |
| S2 | W3 retrieval: quality dimensions; metadata |
| S3 | (a) LIS vs LIMS and BMS destinations · (b) the exchange path · (c) interoperability levels with annotated read-only examples |
| S4 | One discrepancy classified → consequence → control |
| S5 | Annotate an exchange diagram (formative evidence, 4.2) |
| S6 | gov.sg HIA explainer, NEHR contribution section |
| S7 | 8 items |
| S8 | Role and success criteria |
 
**9. Practical.** *Role:* laboratory informatics liaison working with the clinic. *Materials:* synthetic side-by-side source (LIS) and destination (clinic EHR, shared-record view) records; simplified read-only message extracts. *P3:* classify two discrepancies together. *P5:* remaining discrepancies with level, consequence and control; *privacy decision:* should the haemolysis comment and the clinician's free-text note travel with the result? **Primary artefact:** interoperability comparison table with controls.
*Success criteria:* level justified, not just named · consequence linked to reliability or usability · control placed at the right system boundary · privacy choice distinguishes clinically necessary from unnecessary content.
 
**10. Formative.** Exit ticket: "The message was delivered with no error, but the haemolysis comment is missing at the destination. Is this an interoperability failure, and at which level?" *Misconceptions:* LIMS = LIS; delivered means exchanged; same test name means same test; NEHR holds every record or is HealthHub.
 
**11. Singapore context.** Verified: licensed providers must contribute key health information, incl. lab results, to NEHR; intended effect early 2027. Verify locally used standards and code sets (§8 item 9). NEHR technical architecture excluded.
 
**12. BMS relevance.** LIS in hospital labs; LIMS in research, biopharma and QC labs (graduate destinations).
 
**13. Continuity.** ← W3 within-dataset. → W5 release; W9 community provider contribution; C1.
 
**14. Alignment review.** **Green**; Amber for standards verification.

### Week 5 — Laboratory Result Validation and Reporting Safety
 
**1. Role.** Closes the laboratory lifecycle; C1 follows. **Lens:** identifiers as an identity check before release.
 
**2. Central Question.** Should Patient A's raised potassium on a haemolysed sample be released, and who decides?
 
**3. Big Idea.** Release is a professional judgement supported by rules. Autoverification handles the expected, people handle the uncertain, and every decision must be traceable.
 
**4. WLOs**
 
| WLO | MLO | Level | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 Distinguish analytical QC from validation of individual patient results | MLO1 | Analyse | OAL check |
| 5.2 Apply supplied validation rules to identify results for acceptance, review or escalation | MLO2; MLO1 | Apply | Result-review worksheet |
| 5.3 Explain how reference intervals, critical values, delta checks and context support review | MLO1; MLO3 | Understand | Result-review worksheet |
| 5.4 Justify why some results should not be released automatically | MLO2; MLO5 | Evaluate | Result-review worksheet |
 
**5. Prerequisites.** IQC/EQA (§8 item 8 — if not previously taught, add a labelled optional refresher; if essential, flag workload); reference intervals; haemolysis effect on potassium; W3–W4.
 
**Key concepts (5):** analytical QC vs individual-result validation · validation rules (flags, critical values, delta, plausibility, context) · autoverification vs manual review · hold, escalation and critical-result notification (recipient verification, read-back, documentation) · amendments and traceability.
 
**6. GenAI.** Boundary: AI excluded from release decisions.
 
**7. PAIR.** Reflection only (§2).
 
**8. OAL**
 
| Seg | Content |
|---|---|
| S1 | Case: raised K on haemolysed sample; previous result available |
| S2 | W3–W4 retrieval |
| S3 | (a) QC vs result validation (four-way preview) · (b) rule types · (c) autoverification, human review, critical-result notification |
| S4 | Apply a teaching ruleset to 3 results step by step |
| S5 | Branching accept / hold / escalate scenarios |
| S6 | Critical-result communication guidance (e.g. Royal College of Pathologists; verify) |
| S7 | 8 items |
| S8 | Ruleset preview; success criteria |
 
**9. Practical.** *Role:* medical technologist at the result-review bench. *Materials:* 8 synthetic results incl. Patient A; previous results; **teaching ruleset** labelled "not a real laboratory protocol"; critical-result notification protocol. *P3:* first 3 cases together. *P5:* remaining cases incl. haemolysed potassium and identity confirmation; *privacy decision:* minimum information shared by phone and recipient verification; 10-min AI boundary comparison. **Primary artefact:** result-review worksheet.
*Success criteria:* decision consistent with the supplied rules · evidence cited per case · uncertainty acknowledged · no diagnosis · documentation fields complete.
*Answer-guide note:* the model answer for the haemolysed potassium depends on the teaching ruleset; real-laboratory practice varies.
 
**10. Formative.** Exit ticket: "Why might a result that passes every autoverification rule still need review by a person?" *Misconceptions:* an abnormal flag means an error; passing QC means every result is correct; autoverification removes human accountability.
 
**11. Singapore context.** Optional ISO 15189 content (accreditation body and current edition to verify, §8 item 14).
 
**12. BMS relevance.** Core BMS professional task; professional-boundary reflection (validate, do not diagnose).
 
**13. Continuity.** ← W3 haemolysis comment; W4 lost context. → W6 four-way distinction; C1.
 
**14. Alignment review.** **Green**; Amber for prerequisite item 8.

### Week 6 — Healthcare Data Analytics, Visualisation and Decision Support
 
**1. Role.** From patient-level to population-level; outputs as claims to evaluate. C1 may fall this week; it must not displace the practical (`04` §3.1). Feeds C2.
 
**2. Central Question.** When a dashboard and an alert both say something about Patient A and the clinic cohort, which conclusions can we trust?
 
**3. Big Idea.** Analytical and decision-support outputs are claims. Evaluate them against the data, the question and the context before acting.
 
**4. WLOs**

|---|---|---|---|

| 6.3 Evaluate a dashboard or analytical output | MLO3 | Evaluate | Output critique |
| 6.4 Distinguish QC, result validation, population analytics and CDS | MLO3; MLO1 | Analyse | OAL four-way check |
| 6.5 Evaluate a CDS or AI-generated output | MLO3 | Evaluate | Output critique |
 
**5. Prerequisites.** Basic statistics (descriptive measures by retrieval only); sensitivity/specificity (§8 item 8); W5 rules.
 
**Key concepts (5; grouping approved 12 Nov 2026):** descriptive summaries and chart choice · misleading presentation and dashboard critique · four-way distinction · evaluating alerts (sensitivity, specificity, PPV and prevalence) · de-identification and re-identification risk. *Bias and limitations are taught as evaluation criteria within concepts 2 and 4, not as separate concepts.*
 
**6. GenAI.** Augment (critique option).
 
**7. PAIR.** Reflection on a supplied AI interpretation (§2).
