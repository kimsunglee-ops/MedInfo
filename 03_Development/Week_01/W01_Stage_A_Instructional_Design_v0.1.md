## 3. Key concepts and boundaries
 
**Key concepts (5 — at the T&L Design limit):**
1. Medical informatics: the use of data, information and systems to support healthcare and laboratory work.
2. Data → information → knowledge, using laboratory examples.
3. The test-request-to-result journey (stages from order to clinical use).
4. Stakeholders and information hand-offs.
5. Confidentiality, integrity and availability as properties of patient information.
**Context, not concepts to master:** the Singapore orientation map (§8). Assessed at recognition level only; depth is Week 9.
 
**Include:** the five concepts; a simplified, accurate Singapore map; why laboratory information matters to decisions; the AI confidentiality rule.
 
**Exclude:** system functions (Week 2); LIS vs LIMS (Week 4); pre-, intra- and post-analytical error classification (Week 3); legal detail (Week 8); clinical interpretation of results.

## 6. Practical (120 min)
 
**Activity:** Map the journey of Patient A's laboratory test (Part 1 of the two-week artefact).
**Mode:** face-to-face classroom; ≤ 25 students; groups of 3–4.
**Roles (one lens per student):** ordering doctor, phlebotomist or clinic nurse, medical technologist, clinic administrator. Groups of 3 combine the last two.
 
| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| Problem | Patient A's renal panel and HbA1c have been ordered. Where could the result go wrong before the doctor acts on it? |
| Data / information | Case card; stage cards (unordered); role cards; blank A3 journey template; risk symbols (error, delay, loss) |
| Student task | Sequence the stages; for each, record who acts and what information is created or used; mark hand-offs and risk points; mark where confidentiality and integrity matter |
| Decision | Privacy decision: which stakeholders genuinely need to see the result, and which do not |
| Justification | Group writes 3–5 sentences explaining why an instrument value alone is not a reliable result |
| Reflection | Individual exit ticket |
 
**Timing (T&L Design §2.2)**
 
| Segment | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Entry check | 10 | Two lowest-scoring quiz items discussed |
| Briefing | 10 | Scenario, roles, success criteria |
| Guided task | 35 | Order → collection → transport and receipt → testing; lecturer models the first stage, then fades |
| Checkpoint | 10 | Peer swap: each group checks another's map against criteria 1–3 |
| Independent task | 35 | Validation → reporting → clinician receives → action or follow-up; risk points; privacy decision; justification |
| Debrief | 15 | Model map; common errors; privacy decisions compared; preview of Week 2 systems layer |
| Exit ticket | 5 | Individual |
| | **120** | |
 
**Primary artefact:** information-journey map (retained and extended in Week 2).
 
**Success criteria (draft; to match rubric wording in Stage B)**
1. Main stages from order to clinical use are shown in sequence.
2. Each stage names who acts and the information created or used.
3. At least four hand-offs are marked.
4. At least three risk points are classified as error, delay or loss, each with a consequence.
5. Points where confidentiality and integrity matter are marked.
6. The privacy decision is justified.
7. The explanation shows why an analyser value alone is not a reliable result.
**Exit ticket:** "Name one hand-off where a correct analyser value could still become an unreliable result for the doctor. Explain why in one sentence."
 
**Professional boundary:** students describe information flow; they do not interpret Patient A's results.

## 9. Biomedical relevance
 
- Students already know specimen handling from laboratory modules; this week reframes it as **information** that can be lost, delayed or corrupted.
- Graduates in clinical laboratories handle hand-offs daily (receipt, identity checks, reporting); graduates in research or industry laboratories face the same chain with samples instead of patients (Week 4).
- Establishes the professional stance used all module: the BMS role is to make results reliable and safe to use, not to diagnose.

## 10. Formative assessment blueprint
 
Items are written in Stage C to the T&L Design §6.2 standard.
 
| Item type | Target | WLO | Bloom | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | Definitions of data, information, knowledge | 1.2 | Remember/Understand | Quiz |
| Application | Classify laboratory examples (3 items) | 1.2 | Apply | LO2 sort; quiz |
| Application | Order stages of the journey | 1.3 | Apply | Quiz |
| Misconception | "HealthHub is the NEHR" / "NEHR holds every record" | 1.1 | Understand | LO4; quiz |
| Misconception | "If QC passed, the result is reliable" | 1.4 | Understand | Quiz; debrief |
| Higher-order | Short scenario: choose the hand-off whose failure most threatens patient safety and justify | 1.4 | Analyse | Quiz (1–2 items) |
| Role check | Explain one way informatics supports laboratory operations | 1.1 | Understand | Quiz |
 
Quiz: 8 items; at least one per WLO; 3 at Apply or above (meets the one-third rule).
 
**Likely misconceptions to target**
- Medical informatics means IT support or computers.
- A numerical value is already information or knowledge.
- A result is reliable if the analyser and its QC are working.
- HealthHub is the NEHR; NEHR holds all records.
- Confidentiality means nobody sees the data, rather than only the right people.
- Synapxe is a regulator.
