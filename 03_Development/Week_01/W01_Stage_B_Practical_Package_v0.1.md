# Week 1 — Stage B: Practical Package
 
**Practical title:** Map the journey of a laboratory test (Part 1 of a two-week artefact)
**Module:** Medical Informatics (Year 3 BMS, Ngee Ann Polytechnic)
**Version:** 0.1 (1 November 2026)
**Status:** Draft for approval at design gate B
**Baseline:** `W01_Stage_A_Instructional_Design_v0.1.md` (approved); T&L Design v0.1.1 §2.2
**Delivery:** face-to-face classroom; ≤ 25 students; groups of 3–4 (6–7 groups); paper A3 templates (default — see §14)
 
All patients, places and identifiers are fictional. Workflows are generic and vendor-neutral.
 
---
 
## 1. Purpose
 
Students build an information-journey map for one laboratory test and use it to show that a correct analyser value can still become an unreliable result. The map is the base layer for Week 2.
 
## 2. Learning outcomes
 
| WLO | Outcome | Evidence in this practical |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3 | Map the main stages through which a laboratory test request becomes a reported result | Sequenced stages on the map |
| 1.4 | Identify key stakeholders, information hand-offs and potential information risks | Hand-offs, classified risk points, privacy decision, exit ticket |
| 1.1, 1.2 | Supported (OAL-assessed) | Debrief discussion |
 
## 3. Case scenario (student-facing case card)
 
> **Patient A** (fictional) is an adult with type 2 diabetes attending the outpatient clinic at **Riverside General Hospital** (fictional). At today's review, the doctor orders a **renal panel** (including potassium) and an **HbA1c**. Blood is taken in the clinic and sent to the hospital laboratory. The results will return to the doctor, who will decide on follow-up.
>
> You will **not** interpret Patient A's results. Your job is to follow the information.
 
## 4. Student roles
 
Each student takes one role card and speaks for that role throughout. In groups of 3, one student holds both the clinic administrator and phlebotomist cards.
 
| Role | What you need | What you create | What worries you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordering doctor | The right result, for the right patient, in time to decide | The test order, clinical details, urgency, follow-up decision | Results that never arrive, arrive late, or are misread |
| Phlebotomist / clinic nurse | A clear order and a positively identified patient | Labelled specimens, collection time, your identity as collector | Labelling the wrong patient; unclear orders |
| Medical technologist | Specimens that match the request and are fit to test | Receipt record, laboratory number, measured values, review decisions | Unlabelled or mismatched specimens; missing context |
| Clinic administrator | Correct registration details; knowing where reports go | Patient registration, appointment, report routing to the right doctor | Wrong patient details; reports sent to the wrong place |
 
## 5. Background information (on the back of the case card)
 
- A **hand-off** is any point where information or a specimen passes between people or places.
- **Risk types:** Error (wrong information) · Delay (right information, too late) · Loss (information missing or not received).
- **Confidentiality, integrity, availability** (from OAL LO5).
- Systems are named by role only this week (e.g. "where the order is placed"). Week 2 adds what they do.

## 8. A3 journey template
 
Stages run left to right. Rows:
 
| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| A. Stage | Stage card placed here |
| B. Who acts | Role(s) at this stage |
| C. Information created or used | Key items, short phrases |
| D. Hand-off to | Next person or place (arrow) |
| E. Risk | Symbol + one-line consequence |
| F. C / I | Markers where confidentiality or integrity matters |
| G. Systems (Week 2) | **Leave blank** — completed next week |
 
Groups photograph the map at the end; the lecturer keeps the A3 sheet for Week 2.
 
## 9. Student instructions and worksheet
 
### Guided task (35 min) — stages 1–6
1. In your roles, place stage cards 1–6 in order on row A.
2. For each stage, complete rows B and C.
3. Draw each hand-off on row D.
4. Mark at least one risk point on row E, with its type and consequence.
The lecturer models stage 1 with the whole class, then circulates.
 
### Checkpoint (10 min)
Swap maps with another group. Check criteria 1–3 (§16). Write one question or suggestion on a sticky note and return the map.
 
### Independent task (35 min) — stages 7–10
5. Complete rows A–D for stages 7–10.
6. Total at least **four hand-offs** and at least **three risk points** across the whole map, using all three risk types at least once.
7. Add C and I markers (row F).
8. Complete worksheet parts 1 and 2.
**Worksheet**
 
**Part 1 — Privacy decision**
 
| Stakeholder | Needs to see Patient A's result values? (Yes / No / Only if…) | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering doctor | | |
| Covering doctor in the same clinic | | |
| Medical technologist | | |
| Clinic administrator | | |
| Porter or courier | | |
| Patient A | | |
| Patient A's adult child | | |
 
**Part 2 — Justification (3–5 sentences)**
Explain why the number produced by the analyser is not, on its own, a reliable result for Patient A's doctor. Refer to at least two points on your map.
 
**Exit ticket (individual, 5 min)**
Name one hand-off where a correct analyser value could still become an unreliable result for the doctor. Explain why in one sentence.
 
### Extension challenge (groups that finish early)
Which single hand-off on your map would you redesign first to protect Patient A, and why? Consider how likely the failure is and how serious its consequence would be.
 
## 10. Expected responses (answer guide)
 
### Model map (abridged)
 
| # | Stage | Who acts | Information | Hand-off to | Example risk (type → consequence) | C / I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Test ordered | Doctor | Identity, tests, clinical details, urgency, requesting doctor | Collector (order) | Wrong or missing test (error → test not done or wrong test) | I |
| 2 | Identified and collected | Phlebotomist / nurse | Identity check, labels, collection time, collector | Courier (specimens + request) | Wrong patient's labels (error → result filed to wrong patient) | I |
| 3 | Transported | Porter / courier | Specimens, dispatch time | Laboratory receipt | Specimen lost (loss → recollection, delay); late arrival (delay → specimen may be unsuitable) | C |
| 4 | Received and registered | Lab staff | Label–request match, suitability, receipt time, lab number | Processing | Mismatch rejected (delay → recollection); collection time missing (loss) | I |
| 5 | Prepared | Medical technologist | Processing record, specimen comments | Analysis | Specimen mix-up (error) | I |
| 6 | Analysed | Medical technologist + analyser | Values with units, QC status | Review | Value correct but not linked to context (loss) | I |
| 7 | Reviewed | Technologist; senior / pathologist | Reference intervals, previous results, decision | Reporting | Context ignored at review (error) | I |
| 8 | Reported | Laboratory | Values, units, ranges, flags, comments | Doctor | Sent to wrong doctor (loss / C); comment dropped (error) | C, I |
| 9 | Received | Doctor | Notification; viewed result | Patient / follow-up | Urgent result not seen in time (delay) | C |
| 10 | Acted on | Doctor; patient | Decision, documentation, patient informed | — | Follow-up missed (loss) | C |
 
**Hand-offs expected (≥ 4):** doctor → collector; collector → courier; courier → laboratory; laboratory → doctor; doctor → patient. Within-laboratory hand-offs (analysis → review) are also acceptable.
 
### Privacy decision — expected reasoning
 
| Stakeholder | Expected answer | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering doctor | Yes | Needs the result to decide |
| Covering doctor, same clinic | Only if involved in Patient A's care | Need is tied to the care role, not job title |
| Medical technologist | Yes, for testing and review | Cannot perform or check the test otherwise |
| Clinic administrator | No — needs to know a report is available and where it goes | Routing does not require the values |
| Porter / courier | No | Handles specimens, not results |
| Patient A | Yes | The information is about them |
| Adult child | Only with Patient A's consent | Family status alone does not create a need |
 
Credit any answer that applies **need for the role** consistently and justifies exceptions.
 
### Justification — exemplar
"The analyser value is only reliable for the doctor if it belongs to Patient A, came from a suitable, correctly labelled specimen, was reviewed in context and reached the right doctor in time. On our map, a labelling error at collection would attach a correct value to the wrong patient, and a report sent to a doctor who is no longer responsible would mean the result is never acted on. A number can be analytically correct and still be wrong, late or lost for the patient."
 
### Exit ticket — acceptable answers
Any hand-off with a valid reason, e.g. "Collection → laboratory: if the tube is labelled with the wrong patient, the correct value is reported for the wrong person." Weak: naming a stage without a reason; describing an analyser fault (that is analytical error, not a hand-off).
 
## 11. Facilitator guide
 
### Before class
- Print one set of materials per group (§6); add 2 spare sets.
- Review OAL quiz analytics; pick the two lowest-scoring items for the entry check.
- Prepare a model of stage 1 on a projected or demonstration A3.
### Run sheet
 
| Time | Segment | Lecturer actions |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Entry check | Discuss two weak quiz items; correct the "QC passed = reliable result" misconception if present |
| 10–20 | Briefing | Read the case; assign roles; show success criteria; remind: no interpretation of results |
| 20–55 | Guided task | Model stage 1 aloud (who acts, information, hand-off, one risk); fade support by stage 3; prompt quiet roles to speak from their card |
| 55–65 | Checkpoint | Run the swap; listen for groups missing hand-offs |
| 65–100 | Independent task | Circulate; ask "What information travels here?" and "What if this arrived late?"; release extension to fast groups |
| 100–115 | Debrief | Questions below; compare two contrasting maps; preview Week 2 (row G) |
| 115–120 | Exit ticket | Collect slips; photograph maps; keep A3 sheets |
 
### Debrief questions
1. Where did most groups place risk? Why do hand-offs attract risk?
2. Which risk on your map would harm Patient A most, and which is most likely?
3. The analyser's QC was in range all day. Does that make Patient A's result reliable? (Expected: no — QC covers the analytical process only; Week 5 develops this.)
4. Who did you decide should **not** see the values, and why? Did any group disagree?
5. Row G is blank. What do you expect to add next week?
### Signs of difficulty and responses
 
| Sign | Response |
|---|---|
| Stages listed but no information recorded | Ask each role: "What do you write, read or check here?" |
| Every risk labelled "error" | Ask: "Could the right information arrive too late, or not at all?" |
| Group interprets the potassium or HbA1c values | Redirect: "Our job is the journey, not the diagnosis." |
| Systems drawn in detail | Park them in row G for next week |
 
## 12. Common misconceptions
 
| Misconception | Correction used in debrief |
|---|---|
| The result is reliable if the analyser is working | Reliability depends on the whole chain: identity, specimen, context, delivery |
| Risks only occur inside the laboratory | Many risks sit at hand-offs before and after the laboratory |
| Confidentiality means nobody should see results | It means only those who need them for their role |
| The clinic administrator needs the values to route the report | Routing needs to know that a report exists and its destination |
| Family members are entitled to results | Only with the patient's consent |

## 14. Safety, privacy and accessibility
 
- **Privacy:** fictional case only; no real names, hospitals or identifiers; students must not draw on placement experiences involving identifiable patients.
- **Accessibility:** symbols differ by shape; case card available in large print and digital text; roles can be reallocated for students who prefer not to speak; maps can be completed on a laptop if a student cannot write on A3.
- **Tool decision:** paper A3 is the default because the Stage A gate did not name a digital tool. A digital whiteboard can replace it without changing the task.

## 16. Success criteria and formative rubric
 
Formative only; not graded. Criteria wording matches OAL LO6-S2.
 
| # | Criterion | Not yet | Meets | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Main stages from order to clinical use shown in sequence | Stages missing or out of order | All 10 stages in order | Stages in order with a brief note on purpose |
| 2 | Each stage names who acts and the information created or used | Several stages lack people or information | Every stage has both | Information is specific (e.g. collection time, lab number) |
| 3 | At least four hand-offs marked | Fewer than four | Four or more | Includes within-laboratory and laboratory-to-patient hand-offs |
| 4 | At least three risk points, classified with consequences | Fewer than three, or unclassified | Three or more, all three types used | Consequences linked to patient safety |
| 5 | Confidentiality and integrity points marked | Absent or random | Plausible C and I markers | Markers explained in discussion |
| 6 | Privacy decision justified | No reasons | Need-for-role applied consistently | Exceptions (e.g. consent) handled explicitly |
| 7 | Explanation of why an analyser value alone is not reliable | Repeats definition only | Refers to two map points | Distinguishes analytical correctness from reliability for the patient |
